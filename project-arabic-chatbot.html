Dr. Sherine

A conversational assistant that answers personal questions from a corpus of classical Arabic texts, in Egyptian dialect, by text or by voice. The chat interface was never the hard part. Making retrieval work across a fully diacritized Classical Arabic corpus, when users type undiacritized colloquial Egyptian, was.

What it does

The behaviour that matters here is refusal and fidelity, not fluency. A confident wrong answer about scripture is worse than no answer.

Quotes scripture exactlyQuran and hadith are reproduced verbatim in Classical Arabic and only then explained in dialect. Paraphrasing scripture into dialect is explicitly forbidden.
Admits ignoranceWhen the retrieved passages do not cover the question, it says so plainly instead of producing a fluent, unverifiable answer.
Author and book scoping"What does al-Ghazali say about…" restricts retrieval to that author's work and requires the answer to quote his own wording.
Any language in, same language outBut scripture is never translated. It is quoted in the original and explained in the user's language.
Voice in, voice outA recorded question is transcribed, answered through the same pipeline, and spoken back in a cloned voice.
Accounts, sessions and quotasEmail verification, device fingerprinting, trusted devices, and per-role session and message limits.

See it work

Answers are short by design, two or three sentences ending in a follow-up question. Scripture is quoted verbatim first and only then explained in dialect. Watch what happens on the last question, where the corpus has nothing to offer.

Four bugs worth the write-up

In each case the symptom pointed somewhere other than the cause. Three of the four looked like "the retriever is weak" — the most common and most misleading diagnosis in RAG work.

A regex that deleted Arabic lettersThe diacritics-stripping range ran continuously to U+06E8, which swallows U+06E5–U+06E6 — small waw and small ya, real letters. الرازي normalised to an empty string, so any query naming al-Razi searched for nothing. Split the range with a deliberate gap, verified against Unicode's nonspacing-mark category for the whole Arabic block.
False "I don't know" answersAbout 56% of real questions describe the shape of the answer, not its topic — "give me a hadith that talks about marriage". The lexical query required hadith and talks about to co-occur with the topic, and no source chunk narrates "here is a hadith about…". Fix: strip meta-request words from the lexical query only. Embeddings tolerate them; lexical search does not. The same word is noise in one system and signal in the other.
An author name is an identifier, not textScoped queries failed because they required the literal word الرازي inside the matched chunk — but a chunk from al-Razi's own book never repeats his name. Author aliases now scope the query and are excluded from the words required to appear in the text.
The hub chunkOne chunk was the top result for three unrelated questions: its embedding sits near the centre of the corpus's semantic space, so it became the default whenever nothing scored strongly. Not a filter bug — a chunking problem. I shipped the diagnosis and four costed options rather than a rushed fix.

Two things I would defend

Normalisation carries everythingThe corpus is diacritized Classical Arabic (مِعْرَاجُ); users type plain Egyptian (معراج). Byte for byte these never match, so lexical search fires essentially never without normalisation on both sides. The function is duplicated in the index builder and the query path, and if the two drift, retrieval degrades silently instead of erroring — the most dangerous class of bug in a retrieval system.
Three things I built and deletedA keyword-boost layer that worked and cost 78 seconds per request, reverted two commits later and rebuilt properly as an FTS5 index. In-memory BM25, narrowed from nine books to one when RAM ran out, then replaced entirely. A metrics library that errored on every request, dropped after two failed version pins — both still visible in the history.

What I am not claiming

The hub-chunk investigation also found a case where the model produced a confident, detailed answer while most retrieved chunks were off-topic — evidence it was drawing on general knowledge rather than the sources. That is the exact failure the whole design exists to prevent, and it was still happening at that point.

The 100-question evaluation set is scored, but it is my own harness on my own corpus, not an external benchmark. Treat it as a regression guard, not proof of quality against anyone else's system.
