Rafeeq Smart Wheel Kit

Imported smart wheelchairs cost more than most Egyptian families can pay. Rafeeq is a kit that upgrades the manual chair someone already owns, adding autonomous navigation, health monitoring and Arabic voice control that keeps working with no internet. My work was the AI and RAG system and the mobile application.

What it does

The assistant is only useful if it knows the specific patient, not general health advice.

Knows the patient's actual situationLatest mood, pain and sleep check-in; active medications with dosage and frequency; next appointment with doctor and location; and 7-day alarm compliance broken into taken, pending and missed with the specific alarm names.
Reads live vital signsHeart rate, SpO₂ and temperature from the chair's sensors, folded into the conversation context.
Generates a PDF medical reportOn request, detected from natural Arabic phrasing rather than a button.
A separate caregiver assistantIts own persona and prompt, oriented to monitoring: summarises every linked patient, flags missed alarms and upcoming appointments, and includes caregiver-burnout guidance as an explicit task.
Offline Egyptian Arabic voice controlfaster-whisper INT8 on a Raspberry Pi 5, reaching 87% command accuracy at 0.9s latency with no network.
Autonomous indoor navigationLiDAR and depth-camera SLAM with ROS 2 Nav2, and a three-tier control stack from Raspberry Pi through Arduino Mega to STM32.

Problems I solved

The constraint that shaped everything: a wheelchair cannot depend on a network.

Everything critical runs on the chair

Constraint

A mobility device that stops responding when the connection drops is not a mobility device. Cloud speech recognition would be more accurate and completely unsuitable.

Decision

INT8-quantized speech recognition running locally on a Raspberry Pi 5, and a three-tier control stack that converts a spoken command into motor movement in under 20ms without leaving the device.

Cost of the decision

87% command accuracy, lower than a cloud model would reach on the same audio. That is the right trade for this device: a command that works without a network beats a more accurate one that only works with signal.

Cost as a design constraint, not an afterthought

Problem

The target user cannot pay for an imported smart wheelchair. A cheaper version of the same architecture is still too expensive.

Approach

Retrofit rather than replace: the kit upgrades a chair the family already owns. Hardware cost was cut further by repurposing hoverboard motors rather than sourcing purpose-built drives.

Result

A target retail price 60 to 70% below imported alternatives, and a $1,600 grant from Egypt's Ministry of Higher Education, selected from projects across all Egyptian universities.

What I am not claiming

This is a team graduation project. The AI and RAG system and the Flutter application are my work; the hardware and control layers were shared with teammates.

The current two-motor arrangement cannot perform normal left-right turning. That is a real limitation of the build as it stands, not a solved problem.
