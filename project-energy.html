Energy Forecasting & Battery Dispatch

Gradient-boosted day-ahead load forecasting feeding a rolling-horizon Model Predictive Controller that solves a linear program at every step to schedule a 16 kWh battery against a 9 kWp rooftop array, priced against the real Italian time-of-use tariff.

What it does

The objective is measured in euros on a bill, not in forecast error. Those are not the same thing.

Forecasts 96 quarter-hour steps a dayTrained on a full prior year and evaluated on held-out target months.
Models the tariff correctlyThree Italian price bands whose boundaries depend on hour of day, day of week and the public-holiday calendar — holidays bill at the cheapest weekend rate.
Re-solves at every stepA linear program over a 24-hour lookahead horizon, re-solved as new information arrives.
Simulates against physical realityPlans are re-checked against actual load, actual PV, true state-of-charge limits and the grid cap, so reported bills reflect what the battery could really have done.
Benchmarked against three referencesThe site's real recorded controller, a no-battery counterfactual, and a perfect-foresight oracle.
Generalizes to an unseen siteThe same trained pipeline was applied to a second building it had never seen.

Two things worth the write-up

A validation score too good to be trueAn early model reported 0.79 kW validation RMSE. It was an artifact — the feature set was leaking the answer, with information from the target window reachable at prediction time. Rebuilt with honest lags from the previous day and rolling statistics over day-plus windows. The new number was worse and real. A forecasting result that beats expectations deserves to be treated as a bug report until proven otherwise.
Measuring the forecast in money, not errorError metrics do not tell you what a forecast is worth: a model can look worse on NRMSE and still schedule a battery better, because the optimizer only cares about specific hours. So I built a perfect-information oracle — the same optimizer given the true future — and measured the gap in euros. Forecast-driven MPC landed within €0.33 of that ceiling, which means the remaining error costs well under a euro a month and further forecasting work here would be effort on a solved problem.

What I am not claiming

This was a two-person team over three days. Work was joint across forecasting, the optimizer and the evaluation harness; I handled repository and integration, and all commits are under my account, which overstates authorship if read naively.

The euro figures are simulation against recorded site data, not a deployed controller's realised savings.
