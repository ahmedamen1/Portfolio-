Your opening, in 60 seconds

They will open with "tell me about yourself". Say this, then stop talking.

Say roughly this

I am Ahmed Amin, a Communications and Information Engineering graduate from Zewail City, Cum Laude with a 3.64 GPA. For the past nine months I have been an AI Engineer at Takhail, where I build Arabic retrieval systems and LLM assistants that run in production for real clients.

The work I am proudest of is a paper published at IEEE CSCloud last year, where we put fifteen small fine-tuned adapters on one quantized model and beat GPT-4o on CFA exam questions. That is the thread through everything I do: making AI cheap and reliable enough to actually deploy.

In September I start a fully funded MSc in AI for Science at AIMS South Africa on a Google DeepMind scholarship. I am interested in this internship because data governance and data quality is the part of AI work I keep finding matters most, and the part most people skip.

Questions they will probably ask

Click each one. The answers are yours, drawn from work you have actually done, so you are recalling rather than inventing.

Why this internship? Why SKYFIVE?

They want to know you are not applying everywhere at random.

Most of my work has been on the AI side, and what I keep running into is that the model is rarely the problem. Retrieval is. Data quality is. I once spent weeks on what looked like a weak retriever and the actual cause was a regex deleting real Arabic letters from the index. This role is explicitly about data governance and data quality supporting AI, which is the part I have learned matters most and want to get formally good at. Before the call: read their website and add one specific sentence about what SKYFIVE does. Generic answers are obvious.
Walk me through a project you are proud of

Pick FinVerse Agent — it is published, measurable, and shows efficiency thinking.

We built a financial AI agent for a contest. The obvious approach is one fine-tuned model per task, but fifteen tasks means fifteen deployments. Instead we put fifteen small LoRA adapters on a single 4-bit quantized LLaMA-3.1-8B, kept them all in memory so switching is cheap, and added a routing loop that picks the adapter, runs it, and verifies the answer before returning it. We got 93.07% on CFA Level I against GPT-4o's 88.10%, and first place in the contest. I was second author of four.

If they push for detail: the Verifier matters because in finance a fluent answer with an implausible number is worse than a refusal — it looks usable.

Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem

Use the hub chunk. It shows diagnosis, not just coding.

Users were getting the right book but the wrong passage. My first instinct was that the filter was broken. I tested five questions systematically and found the filter was fine — the same chunk was winning three completely unrelated queries. Its embedding sat near the centre of the corpus, so it became the default whenever search found nothing strong. The real cause was chunking, upstream of the code I was looking at. I wrote up four candidate fixes with their costs and deliberately changed no code that pass, because the diagnosis was the deliverable.
Tell me about a mistake, or something that went wrong

Do not dodge this. Use the 143 images — it is genuinely honest and shows you correct yourself.

I told a client twice that their buildings had no good imagery and recommended they commission more renders. I was wrong. 143 images were sitting on disk but missing from the description file, so they were never indexed and no query could reach them. I had diagnosed "the data doesn't exist" from inside the system's own index without checking the filesystem. I generated descriptions from the structured filenames and re-ingested from 702 to 845. It is the mistake I would most want back, and it changed how I verify a data gap.

Why this works: it is a data governance failure, told honestly, in an interview about data governance.

How comfortable are you with SQL and databases?
Comfortable in production rather than in theory. I use PostgreSQL with pgvector and FTS5 for hybrid search, MongoDB for conversation state, and Redis for caching and queues, across four deployed systems. I took Database Management Systems and got an A. Where I would want to grow is formal data governance — schema design at organisation scale, lineage, and quality rules as a discipline rather than as things I fix when they break. Brush up before the call: JOINs, GROUP BY, window functions, indexes, and normal forms. A light SQL question is likely.
What is your experience with data quality?

Three concrete stories, all real:

First, a regex that used a Unicode range spanning two real Arabic letters, so a scholar's name normalised to an empty string and every query naming him matched nothing. Second, 143 images that existed on disk but were missing from the ingestion file. Third, an evaluation harness where I found the LLM judge scoring an answer fully faithful while its own context-recall metric read zero — it had certified a hallucination. That is why I keep deterministic checks alongside model-based ones.

If it runs longer, I am happy to discuss remote or part-time arrangements, and I would rather agree that now than surprise anyone later. Check the program dates before the call. Where do you see yourself, or what are you looking to learn?
I want to keep building AI systems that run in production rather than in notebooks, and I want to get properly good at the data layer underneath them. The MSc is an AI for Science track, so I will be strengthening the maths and research side. What I would most like from an internship is exposure to how a larger organisation handles data governance formally, because so far I have learned it by fixing things after they broke.
Do you have questions for us?

Always say yes. See the section below.

What to ask them

Pick three. Asking nothing reads as not being interested.

What does the data landscape look like at SKYFIVE today, and where does the internship sit in it? Shows you think about context, not just tasks.
Is the AI work more analytics and decision support, or building models that go into products? Genuinely useful for you to know, and it is a sharp question.
How do you currently measure data quality, and who owns it when something is wrong? This is the question someone who has actually done the work asks.
What would a successful first three months look like for this intern? Signals you are thinking about delivering, not just being hired.
What are the program dates, and is there flexibility in the arrangement? You need this answer because of September. Ask it plainly.

Traps to avoid

  • Do not claim sole credit for the paper. You are second author of four. Say so — it costs nothing and being caught overstating costs everything.
  • Do not say "zero hallucination" or similar absolutes. Your own evaluation found six unsupported cases. Say "grounded", and describe the guard.
  • Do not hide the September MSc. Raise it yourself, calmly, with options.
  • Do not oversell the SQL. You are strong in applied use, not in formal governance. Saying where you want to grow is a strength in an internship interview.
  • Do not ramble. Answer, give one example, stop. Silence is fine — let them ask the follow-up.
  • Do not read from this page during the call. Read it before. Reading aloud is audible.
