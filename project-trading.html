Trading & Market Assistant

A production conversational platform for an online trading brokerage. A bilingual English and Gulf-Arabic assistant that answers market questions with live data, explains the firm's products from a curated knowledge base, captures leads, and speaks to customers by voice over the web and over the phone.

What it does

A sales assistant for a brokerage has to be right about two very different kinds of fact: what the market did five minutes ago, and what the firm charges.

Live market data per question"Why is gold moving today?" gets a grounded answer with real prices and cited catalysts, not a guess from stale training data. Web search runs per question and merges with technical indicators from a market API.
Product facts from a vector storeAccount tiers, spreads, commissions, deposit and withdrawal rules served from curated content rather than model memory — the difference between a correct quote and an invented one on a sales call.
Writes a regional urban dialectNot Modern Standard Arabic. Enforced by a prompt specification covering a vocabulary map, pronouns, and the specific grammatical tells that mark a non-native speaker.
Talks over the phoneA dedicated tool endpoint backs a third-party voice agent, returning a finished, speakable sentence for it to read aloud.
Renders live charts inlineThe assistant decides when a question deserves a chart, a comparison, or a market-overview widget, and emits it alongside the streamed answer.
Captures and qualifies leadsNames and phone numbers extracted from natural conversation by a cheap model running on every message, surfaced with history on a dashboard.

Problems I solved

The core design question was which subsystem is allowed to answer which kind of question.

Routing between tools and retrieval, by kind of truth

Problem

Live market data cannot go through a knowledge base: it is stale the moment it is indexed. Product facts cannot go through a live feed: the web does not know this firm's commission structure.

Decision

The assistant routes per question between tool calls for market data and retrieval for product facts, so each question is answered by the source that can actually be right about it. Getting this wrong in either direction produces a confident, plausible, wrong answer on a sales call.

A dialect specification, not a dialect instruction

Problem

Asking a model to "reply in Gulf Arabic" produces Modern Standard Arabic with a few dialect words sprinkled in. Native speakers spot it instantly, and on a sales call that costs trust.

Approach

A detailed prompt specification rather than an instruction: a vocabulary map, the correct pronouns, and the specific grammatical tells that mark a non-native speaker. Naming the failure modes explicitly works better than describing the target.

What I am not claiming

This is the project I have the least public measurement for. Line counts and service architecture are read from the repository; I have no published evaluation harness for it comparable to the property-sales work.
