FinVerse Agent

A task-adaptive financial AI system built for the FinAI Contest 2025. Fifteen task-specific LoRA adapters sit on a single 4-bit quantized LLaMA-3.1-8B, with an agentic routing loop that picks the adapter, runs it, and verifies the answer before returning it.

What it does

The premise: a well-trained small model, specialised per task, can beat a much larger general one on finance work.

Planner, Executor, VerifierThe Planner picks the adapter, the Executor attaches it to the frozen base and runs it, and the Verifier applies domain constraints — discrete answer formats for exams, numeric plausibility for calculations — re-routing invalid outputs.
Hybrid adapter routingHeuristic intent cues such as MCQ format and XBRL tokens, embedding similarity to task centroids, and a lightweight supervised classifier over query embeddings.
Adapters chainMulti-stage questions run adapters in sequence, for example XBRL value extraction followed by formula calculation.
Fifteen financial tasksCFA Levels I and II, sentiment, headlines, NER, XBRL tagging and formula work, ConvFinQA, Financial Math, FinanceBench.
Trained under constraintLoRA rank 4 to 8, alpha 16, 8 epochs on an A100 40GB, over 12 hours per adapter, using Unsloth and bitsandbytes on a 4-bit base.
Replicated on a second baseThe same 15 adapters were trained on Qwen-2.5-7B as well, for 30 experiments total.

Problems I solved

One architectural decision carries most of the result.

Fifteen adapters on one model, not fifteen models

Problem

Specialisation usually means one fine-tuned model per task. Fifteen tasks would mean fifteen deployments, fifteen sets of weights in memory, and fifteen things to keep in sync.

Decision

One frozen 4-bit base with fifteen LoRA adapters attached to the key projection layers. All adapters stay pre-loaded in memory so switching is cheap, and a routing cache keyed on query embeddings reuses decisions for repeated or semantically similar queries.

Result

Specialisation without paying for fifteen deployments. On CFA Level I the adapters reach 93.07% against GPT-4o's 88.10%, and on NER 97.24 F1 against BloombergGPT's 60.82.

A Verifier, because a confident wrong number is the failure mode

Problem

In financial reasoning, a fluent answer in the wrong format or with an implausible magnitude is worse than a refusal, because it looks usable.

Design

The Verifier applies domain-specific constraints per task: exam answers must be a discrete option, calculations must be numerically plausible. Invalid outputs are re-routed to an alternate adapter rather than returned.

What I am not claiming

I am the second author of four. The work was a collaboration between NUST Islamabad and Zewail City, and I do not claim sole credit for the paper.

DeepSeek V3 and Gemini 2.0 FL still beat these adapters on raw XBRL tag and value extraction. The advantage here is on exam reasoning, sentiment, NER and compositional numeric work. The paper says so, and so do I.

The paper notes the full agentic routing loop was still under implementation at publication; the reported figures are adapter-level performance.
