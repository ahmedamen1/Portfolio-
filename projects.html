Arabic Advisory Chatbot

Client work · production · 5 months
Assistant

Answers personal questions from a corpus of classical Arabic texts, in Egyptian dialect, by text or voice.

  • Quotes scripture exactly. Verses and hadith are reproduced verbatim in Classical Arabic, then explained in dialect. Paraphrasing scripture is explicitly forbidden.
  • Admits ignorance. When retrieved passages do not cover the question it says so, rather than producing a fluent unverifiable answer.
  • Author and book scoping. "What does al-Ghazali say about..." restricts retrieval to that author and requires his own wording.
  • Any language in, same language out — but scripture is never translated, only quoted and then explained.
  • Voice in, voice out, answered through the same pipeline and spoken back in a cloned voice.
  • Accounts and quotas — email verification, device fingerprinting, trusted devices, per-role session and message limits.
The problem I solved

Users got the right book but the wrong passage. The filter was fine: one chunk sat near the centre of the corpus embedding space and won three unrelated queries by default. A chunking problem, not a code problem. I wrote up four fixes with costs and shipped the diagnosis before touching code.

Property-Sales AI Assistant

Client work · production
Assistant

A bilingual sales assistant for a large mixed-use development. Buyers ask in Arabic or English and get answers with the right drawings attached.

  • Shows the right pictures. Floor plans and renders retrieved alongside the text, scoped to the building under discussion, with images already shown earlier suppressed.
  • Numeric filtering in natural language — "under 90 m²", "between 70 and 100", "أكبر من 120 متر", plus qualitative forms like "big" or "compact", including Arabic-Indic numerals.
  • Resolves follow-ups. "What about the ground floor?" or a bare "yes" resolve against remembered state: last building, last catalogue, last question asked.
  • Refuses out of scope. Competitor questions get a fixed redirect; unknown building codes get "I don't have that, here is the official channel" rather than an invention.
  • Captures leads and powers a sales dashboard with eight endpoints: overview, timeseries, intents, properties, funnel, queries, leads, behaviour.
  • Voice in, voice out, in the buyer's own language.
The trade-off I chose

A guard that checks every claim against context blocks most good answers, because sales language legitimately rephrases. Mine blocks only explicit financial claims — prices, instalments, "sold out". A loose description is recoverable; an invented price is not.

Multi-Channel Sales Assistant

Client work · WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram
Assistant

The same assistant reachable from three messaging platforms, behaving like a well-briefed salesperson in whichever language the customer writes.

  • Language judged per message, so a customer switching mid-conversation is followed rather than corrected.
  • Sends the right pictures without being asked twice. "Show me B1.1" returns that building's exterior render followed by its floor plans.
  • Reports real inventory. "What units are bigger than 70 m?" returns all 176 matching configurations across 19 buildings, grouped by bedroom count, not three illustrative examples.
  • Answers voice notes with voice notes, synthesised in the same dialect.
  • Declines cleanly. Will not invent a building, will not discuss a competitor, and does not attach photos to a pricing question.
The constraint I designed around

Messaging platforms ban business accounts that behave like broadcasters. The rate limits are shaped around per-recipient behaviour rather than a global cap, so genuine conversations stay fast while the pattern never looks automated.

Trading & Market Assistant

Client work · online brokerage
Assistant

A bilingual English and Gulf-Arabic assistant that answers market questions with live data and explains products from a curated store.

  • Live market data per question. "Why is gold moving today?" gets real prices and cited catalysts, not a guess from stale training data.
  • Product facts from a vector store — account tiers, spreads, commissions, deposit rules — the difference between a correct quote and an invented one on a sales call.
  • Writes a regional urban dialect, not Modern Standard Arabic, enforced by a prompt specification covering vocabulary, pronouns and the grammatical tells of a non-native speaker.
  • Talks over the phone. A tool endpoint backs a third-party voice agent, returning a finished speakable sentence.
  • Renders live charts inline, deciding for itself when a question deserves a chart, a comparison or a market-overview widget.
  • Captures and qualifies leads from natural conversation using a cheap model on every message.
The call I made

Live market data cannot go through a knowledge base, and product facts cannot go through a live feed. The assistant routes between tool calls for market data and retrieval for product facts, so each question is answered by the source that can actually be right about it.

FinVerse Agent

IEEE CSCloud 2025 · second author of four
Research

A financial AI agent built for the FinAI 2025 contest. Fifteen task-specific LoRA adapters on a single 4-bit quantized LLaMA-3.1-8B.

  • Planner, Executor, Verifier loop. The Planner picks the adapter, the Executor runs it, and the Verifier checks format and numeric plausibility, re-routing the query when the answer looks wrong.
  • Hybrid adapter routing — heuristic intent cues, embedding similarity to task centroids, and a supervised classifier over query embeddings.
  • Adapters chain for multi-stage questions, such as XBRL value extraction followed by formula calculation.
  • Trained across 15 financial tasks: CFA Levels I and II, sentiment, NER, XBRL tagging and formula work, ConvFinQA, FinanceBench.
The call I made

Fifteen adapters on one base model, not fifteen models. All adapters stay pre-loaded in memory so switching is cheap, and a routing cache keyed on query embeddings reuses decisions for similar questions. Specialisation without paying for fifteen deployments.

Energy Forecasting & Battery Dispatch

Two-person team · 2026
Optimization

Day-ahead load forecasting feeding a rolling-horizon Model Predictive Controller that schedules a 16 kWh battery against a 9 kWp rooftop array.

  • Forecasts 96 quarter-hour steps a day, trained on a full prior year and evaluated on held-out target months.
  • Models the Italian time-of-use tariff correctly — three price bands whose boundaries depend on hour, weekday and the public-holiday calendar, since holidays bill at the cheapest rate.
  • Re-solves a linear program at every step over a 24-hour lookahead as new information arrives.
  • Simulates against physical reality — plans re-checked against actual load, actual PV, true state-of-charge limits and the grid cap, so reported bills reflect what the battery could really have done.
  • Benchmarked against three references: the site's real controller, a no-battery counterfactual, and a perfect-foresight oracle.
  • Generalizes to an unseen site, tested on a second building the pipeline had never seen.
The mistake I caught

An early model showed a validation RMSE of 0.79 kW. It was an artifact: the features were leaking the answer. Rebuilding with honest lags and rolling windows gave a worse number and a real one. I measured against a perfect-information oracle to show exactly what forecast error costs in euros.

Multi-Camera Threat Detection

Sole engineer · training and runtime
Vision

Watches several camera feeds at once for weapons, falls and violence, with alerting and a cloud evidence pipeline.

  • Per-camera detector assignment. An entrance camera can run weapon detection only while a corridor camera runs fall detection only, configured in JSON.
  • Two-stage weapon detection — a detector proposes regions, CLIP verifies each crop against positive and negative prompts before anything counts as a threat.
  • Falls from bounding-box geometry, required to persist across consecutive frames before firing.
  • Violence from 16-frame sequences using a 3D-CNN over a rolling buffer.
  • Automatic evidence escalation — a frame or reconstructed MP4 uploaded to a category-specific cloud folder, made link-accessible, and emailed to security staff.
  • Live dashboard with MJPEG feeds, on-frame HUD, a real-time violence-score meter, and a health panel marking each camera online or offline with FPS and queue depth.
The problem I solved

A detector tuned for recall fires on phones and wallets; tuned for precision it misses real weapons. Both are unacceptable in a school. I run the detector permissively at 0.40, then pass every crop to CLIP scored against positive and explicitly negative prompts — so a phone is actively labelled a phone, not merely "less like a weapon".

Rafeeq Smart Wheel Kit

Graduation project · Lead AI Architect
Hardware

A kit that upgrades the manual wheelchair someone already owns, because imported smart chairs cost more than most Egyptian families can pay.

  • Knows the patient's actual situation — latest mood and pain check-in, active medications with dosage, next appointment, and 7-day alarm compliance broken into taken, pending and missed.
  • Reads live vital signs from the chair's sensors and folds them into the conversation context.
  • Generates a PDF medical report on request, detected from natural Arabic phrasing.
  • A separate caregiver assistant with its own persona, summarising every linked patient, flagging missed alarms, and including burnout guidance as an explicit task.
  • Offline Egyptian Arabic voice control and autonomous indoor navigation with LiDAR and depth-camera SLAM.
The constraint I designed around

A wheelchair cannot depend on a network. Everything critical runs on the chair: INT8 speech recognition on a Raspberry Pi 5, and a three-tier control stack that converts a spoken command to motor movement in under 20ms without leaving the device.
