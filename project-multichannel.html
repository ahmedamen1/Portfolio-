Multi-Channel Sales Assistant

The same assistant reachable from three messaging platforms, behaving like a well-briefed salesperson in whichever language the customer writes. I owned the retrieval and image layer, the multilingual output, and the rate limits that keep a business account from being flagged.

What it does

From the customer's side it should feel like messaging a person who knows the inventory.

Answers in the language they wrote inJudged per message, so a customer switching mid-conversation is followed rather than corrected.
Sends the right pictures"Show me B1.1" returns that building's exterior render followed by its floor plans. A question about a 106 m² apartment returns the actual 106 m² unit plans.
Reports real inventory"What units are bigger than 70 m?" returns all 176 matching configurations across 19 buildings, grouped by bedroom count — not three illustrative examples.

Problems I solved

The two most useful things I found here came from instrumenting what I assumed was working.

A threshold that was silently discarding the entire semantic leg

Symptom

Broad questions returned visibly arbitrary images. The ranking code looked correct.

Measurement

I instrumented the retrieval legs separately. A specific query like "B5.2a floor plan" had a best vector distance of 0.64 to 0.68. "tell me about the project" scored 1.38 to 1.43. The relevance ceiling was 1.35.

Cause

Every image description is about a specific building, so nothing sits semantically close to a question about the development as a whole. For those queries all 36 vector candidates were discarded, leaving retrieval running on keyword overlap alone. That is why results looked random: whichever caption happened to share a word won.

Fix

Raised the ceiling to 1.55, calibrated from those measurements rather than guessed. The guard that keeps images off pricing questions is a separate non-visual term list, which is where that responsibility belongs.

143 images I wrongly reported as missing

What I said

The residential towers appeared to have no good imagery. I reported this to the client twice as a data limitation and recommended sourcing more renders.

What was true

I was wrong. 143 images were sitting on disk but absent from the description CSV, so they were never embedded and no query could reach them — the per-unit floor plans for exactly the buildings I had written off.

Fix

Generated descriptions from the structured filenames, which carry building, tower and unit position, and re-ingested from 702 to 845 embeddings.

Lesson

I diagnosed "the data doesn't exist" from inside the system's own index without checking the filesystem. Confidently reporting a data gap that was actually an ingestion gap is the mistake in this project I would most want back.

Rate limits shaped around how bans actually happen

Constraint

Messaging platforms ban business accounts that behave like broadcasters. A global message cap is the obvious implementation and the wrong one: it throttles genuine conversations while still allowing a burst pattern that looks automated.

Design

Limits are shaped around per-recipient behaviour instead. A single customer in an active conversation gets fast replies; the account-wide pattern never resembles a broadcast.

What I am not claiming

The 176-configuration inventory answer is correct against the structured data I was given. I did not independently audit the client's own inventory records against it.
